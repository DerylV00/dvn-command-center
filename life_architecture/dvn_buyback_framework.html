01 · The Foundation · Know Your Number
The Buyback
Rate.
Every task is a financial decision once you know your number. Spending three hours on a task you could pay someone else $15/hr to do is not productivity. It is a measurable loss.
The Formula
Annual income target ÷ 2,000 hours ÷ 4 = your Buyback Rate per hour
The ÷4 ensures you only buy back at 25% of your earning rate — preserving margin. The formula forces the question: what is my time actually worth, and am I spending it accordingly?

02 · The Map · Where is your time living?
The DRIP
Matrix.
Four quadrants defined by two axes: energy (draining vs. filling) and skill level (low vs. high). Plot every task. Then empty the wrong quadrants systematically.
DELEGATION · Low skill, draining
Delete it. Delegate it. Now.
This is where you start. The quick wins. Outsource for your Buyback Rate or less and immediately free time for higher-value work. Don't wait until you have the perfect person.
REPLACEMENT · High skill, draining
Build a system or a person.
Where successful operators get permanently stuck. You're good at it — but it costs you. The only way out is the Replacement Ladder. Atlas was built specifically to absorb this quadrant for Ken.
INVESTMENT · Low skill, energizing
Learn. Experiment. Don't monetize yet.
Spanish. New relationships. Deferred experiences. The Thailand vision. This quadrant seeds what Production becomes in two to three years. Protect it as learning time, not idle time.
PRODUCTION · High skill, energizing
Protect this at all costs.
This is the zone. Every system, every hire, every delegation exists to create more time here. For Deryl: deep presence, facilitation, listening. For Ken: strategy, relationships, Growth House vision.

03 · The Enemies · What steals time before you notice
The 5 Time
Assassins.
These are the patterns that drain your time before you realize it. Optimus and Atlas flag them by name the moment a signal phrase appears. One flag, one question, then move on.
AssassinWhat it looks likeSignal phraseThe one question
Seagulling Swooping in on someone else's task, taking over, flying away. Looks like helping. Creates dependency. "I just handled it myself." What would have happened if you hadn't stepped in?
Whirlwind So consumed by urgent reactive tasks that important work never gets scheduled. Always busy, never moving. "I've been slammed all week." How much of that was in your Production quadrant?
Perfectionism Doing it yourself because no one else will do it right. Really means: haven't built the playbook or hired correctly. "It's just faster if I do it." What's the cost of that at your Buyback Rate?
Bottlenecking Every decision requires your approval. Nothing moves without you. The team waits constantly. "I need to be on that call." What outcome were you protecting — can that be set in advance?
Nostalgia Staying in tasks you used to be great at but have outgrown. Comfortable. Safe. Below your current level. "I actually enjoy doing this part." Is this the highest and best use of you right now?
04 · The Levels · What kind of trader are you?
The Only 3
Trades.
Most people — even business owners — are permanently stuck at Level 1. The pivot to Level 2 is where time freedom actually begins. Level 3 is the destination.
Level 1 · Employee
Trading TIME for money
Hours in = dollars out. Ceiling is fixed. No leverage. The more you do, the more is expected. This is the default — and it's a trap.
Level 2 · Entrepreneur
Trading MONEY for time
You invest money to free your hours. This is the pivot. It feels counterintuitive — spending to get time back. But this is where leverage begins.
Level 3 · Empire Builder
Trading MONEY for more money
Systems and people work while you sleep. You are no longer trading time for anything. This is the destination. Ken is here. Deryl is building toward it.
05 · The Handoff · You cannot skip rungs
The Replacement
Ladder.
Every task you want to remove yourself from has four rungs. Skip a rung and it breaks. This is the architecture for every delegation — from contractor handoffs to Atlas absorbing Ken's behavioral tracking.
1
I do, I teach
Do the task. Document it while doing it. No skipping — you must understand it fully before you can transfer it. This rung is where the playbook gets written.
2
I do, they watch
They shadow you. They see how you make decisions — not just what steps you follow. Context transfers here, not just process.
3
They do, I guide
They execute. You correct and refine. You're still in it — but not doing it. This rung is where most handoffs stall. Push through it.
4
They do, I review
They own it. You check outcomes, not process. You are free. This is the only rung that actually counts — everything else is progress toward here.
06 · The Shift · How you lead changes everything
Transformational
Leadership.
The move from transactional to transformational is the shift from managing tasks to leading people. One is a ceiling. The other is a multiplier.
Transactional — broken
Tell HOW to do it
Check if done your way
Do it yourself when it's not
→
Transformational — working
Set the OUTCOME
Measure the result
Develop the person
Decision fatigue is real. Every instruction you give is a decision you made for someone else — a decision you cannot use for your own highest-value work. When you lead through outcomes, your team starts asking "Is there a better way?" instead of "How do we do this?"
