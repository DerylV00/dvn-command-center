Blaze Star · Phase Plan · 2026

Build like a Star.
Delegate like a genius.

The Fellowship of Minds phase plan built specifically around the Blaze Star operating system — high intuition, deep empathy, vision-led. Every phase is designed for how you actually work, not how a Steel or Tempo would.

01 — Your Genius Profile

The Blaze Star. Not a pure Blaze.

Understanding the specific sub-profile matters. A pure Blaze magnifies by connecting other people. A Star magnifies differently — by shining light on value through personal brand, voice, and presence. The operating system is distinct. So is the phase plan.

Dynamo Energy
The Idea Power
A constant stream of creative concepts. You see the big picture before anyone else has framed the problem. You rely heavily on intuition — and your intuition is usually right. The danger is mistaking the vision for the plan. Seeing it clearly doesn't mean the path is built. A Dynamo without a Tempo to catch the build gets a graveyard of brilliant starts.
Blaze Energy
The People Power
Unlike a pure Dynamo who creates in isolation, you care deeply about how your ideas affect people. You read rooms instantly. You understand human needs before they're spoken. You naturally champion the well-being of others — which is your greatest gift and your biggest trap. Because you genuinely care, you say yes to everyone. The fix isn't caring less. It's building the structure that lets you care at scale without breaking.
How you magnify
Shining light on value
A pure Blaze magnifies by connecting other people. A Star magnifies by putting their own voice, presence, and personal brand on an idea — and that light draws others in. FOM succeeds when Deryl is the visible face of it. The platform is the product. You are the proof.
Where you win
Front of house, always
Stars fail when they try to do detailed back-office operations. They win when they stay out in front, inspiring the team and the audience. The moment Deryl is debugging n8n workflows instead of talking to Ken, the Star is misplaced.
Your wealth path
The Who over the How
Like all Blaze profiles, the path to scale is finding the right people to build your ideas — not building them yourself. The question is never "how do I build this?" The question is always "who is the right person to build this, and how do I attract them into this mission?"
The Star's shadow — know this before Phase 1
Overwhelming yourself trying to please or rescue everyone.
This isn't a productivity problem. It's an empathy problem. Because you genuinely care about every person in front of you, you say yes before you've assessed whether the yes serves the mission. You take on their energy. You feel responsible for their outcomes. The result is a Blaze Star running at 110% capacity with no margin left for the vision — and the vision is the entire point. The fix per MMP is structural, not motivational: build a team of Tempo and Steel geniuses who handle the details and grounding so you can stay in creating, leading, and caring without burning the engine.
02 — Operating Rules for the Blaze Star

How you build without burning.

These are not generic productivity tips. These are rules derived directly from the Blaze Star operating system — the specific guardrails that keep the Star in front without getting pulled into Steel territory.

01
Short sprints with hard deliverables
Blaze Stars lose energy in long slow builds. Structure work in 2–3 week sprints with a single, visible, tangible output at the end. Not "work on the agent." Ship the Ken onboarding conversation by June 30. The deliverable is the energy source. Without it, you're running on fumes by week three.
02
Visible momentum markers, weekly
The Star's energy is sustained by seeing things move. Build a weekly visible win into every phase — something you can point to, show someone, or send in a message. Not internal progress. External, observable evidence that the mission is advancing. This is also your accountability mechanism — if there's nothing to show on Friday, the week didn't count.
03
Explicit handoff points before boredom hits
The Blaze Star doesn't burn out — they drift. Once the vision is clear and the first proof is built, the energy starts migrating to the next idea. Build handoff points into the plan before you feel the drift — not after. The moment the build phase starts, identify who takes it from there. Don't wait to feel bored. Pre-decide.
04
Never be the one debugging infrastructure
Ollama configuration, Supabase schema migrations, n8n workflow errors, Cloudflare tunnel setup — none of this is Blaze Star work. Every hour spent debugging infrastructure is an hour not spent in conversation with Ken, building the FOM narrative, or attracting the right people into the mission. The moment you're in a terminal troubleshooting a webhook, a Steel is missing from the team.
05
External accountability loop, not internal
Stars are not self-accountable by default — they're accountable to the people they care about. This is not a weakness. Wire your accountability to a real human who will notice and name it when you drift. Ken is a candidate. Jesse Ray is a candidate. The FOM agent you're building is ultimately a candidate — because you built it, you care about it, and you don't want it to catch your own drift.
06
The mission speaks — not the technology
When Deryl talks about FOM, it cannot lead with infrastructure. Stars sell through story and presence. The conversation with Ken is about what his life looks like in 90 days — not about Base.44 or Supabase schemas. The technology is invisible. The transformation is the pitch. This applies to every relationship in the FOM build: Jesse, future community licenses, future investors.

The biggest risk for a Blaze Star building a platform is scope creep into the wrong genius zone. This is the explicit line — drawn before the build starts so it doesn't get redrawn under pressure.

04 — The Phase Plan

Four phases. Built for the Star.

Each phase has a Blaze Star role (what Deryl does), a Steel/Tempo role (what someone else does), a sprint deliverable (the visible win), and a handoff signal (when to transition). No phase lasts longer than 8 weeks before a visible output.

Phase 0
Prove it on yourself
Now → June 30, 2026
Sprint output: Deryl's personal agent running daily for 30 days. One documented insight per week.
The Star builds credibility through lived experience, not theory. Before you can sell Ken on what his agent will do for his life, you need to know what yours has done for yours. This phase is Deryl running his own agent in Base.44 — every day, for 30 days. Not to build the perfect system. To feel what the accountability loop actually does. To notice what the pattern interrupt catches. To collect the language that makes it real when you talk to Ken.
Deryl's work (Star zone)
  • Use the agent every morning — no exceptions
  • Write down what it catches, what it misses
  • Frame the 3 most powerful moments in plain language
  • Tell one person per week what you're building and why
Base.44 handles
  • Agent hosting and infrastructure
  • Context loading — all 5 frameworks
  • Conversation interface
  • Zero setup required — Deryl never touches a terminal
Do not do in Phase 0
  • Buy the Mac Mini yet — premature
  • Design the community agent schema
  • Build anything for Ken yet
  • Pitch FOM publicly before the proof is personal
Handoff signal
30 days of daily use complete 3 documented insights ready to share with Ken Energy is high — not depleted

06 — Accountability Architecture

How the Star stays on mission.

Stars are accountable to people they care about — not to systems or deadlines. The accountability architecture for a Blaze Star wires the commitments to real humans, not calendars. And the agent that Deryl builds is ultimately the most powerful accountability mechanism he has — because he built it, he cares about it, and it knows his patterns.

Weekly rhythm
Friday visible win review
Every Friday, one question: what can I point to that moved this week? Not internal progress. Not "I thought about it." Something observable — a conversation had, a document sent, a system running, an insight documented. If there's nothing to point to, the week was spent in the wrong zone. This is the Star's self-audit. Takes three minutes. Tells you everything.
Human accountability
Ken as the mirror
The Ken pilot creates a natural accountability loop. When Deryl shows up for the weekly check-in call, he cannot show up empty-handed. The agent is running or it isn't. The loop is working or it isn't. Ken's experience is the evidence. This is the Blaze Star's most powerful accountability mechanism — caring about the outcome for someone else. It activates the deep empathy that is your greatest strength.
Phase gate accountability
No phase skipping
The Blaze Star's shadow is the shiny next thing pulling them out of phase before the current one compounds. The phase gates are the guardrail. Phase 2 does not open until the Phase 1 handoff signals are met. Not "mostly met." Met. The Mac Mini doesn't get ordered until Ken has 30 days on the agent. The community layer doesn't open until Ken's outcome is documented. These are hard stops — not suggestions.
The agent as mirror
Deryl's agent catches Deryl
The personal agent running in Base.44 is not just a product prototype. It is Deryl's accountability partner. It knows the Blaze Star's specific drift patterns — the language of avoidance, the signal of overcommitment, the moment energy migrates before the current phase is done. The agent that Deryl builds for others first works on Deryl. This is how the Star proves the product — by living in it.
