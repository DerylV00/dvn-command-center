Magnification wins.

"How can this only be done with me?"

Blaze business type · Chapter 6
Market-based.
Summer season.
"In the summer season of an industry, the point of power moves to market-based businesses. When there is so much choice of products out there, people prefer to gather around like-minded people in communities where they can hear about the latest best products."

Key metrics for a Blaze market-based business: not how many products you sell — but how many customers you have and the lifetime value of all products and services you provide to each. The switching cost increases with time. The community is the moat.

The context · Chapter 5
Orange to Yellow.
The Enterprise prism.
At Orange you are in positive cash flow but still chasing business. You have a strong work ethic but are wondering how to turn all this effort into a higher level of income. The shift to Yellow is from dependence to independence — from chasing the ball to choosing a position where others pass it to you.
Key insight from the source: All flow is made up of projects and processes. Projects grow or enhance flow. Processes maintain flow. To reach Yellow, you need to automate or outsource your processes so you can focus all your time on profitable projects — promotions.

Chapter 4 Action Point · Five Steps to Follow the Flow
Create a job.
Don't find one.
"All successful business owners or investors could start all over again if their business failed because they have built the ability to find out what people need and then deliver against that. They know this skill set is the best form of job security anyone can have today."

These five questions are asked by everyone who successfully moves from Red to Orange. The answers connect you with those already in flow.
1
What are the criteria for the job you are going to create?
›
Set the amount of Superman time you can put toward this goal each week. Set the specific amount of money you want to achieve in that time. Now look at your genius and the current skill sets and experience you have.
"Write a list of the criteria you would want the job you create to meet — where you can feel confident you can add value to the people you will be approaching. What are the activities you would be energized by and confident in doing?"
Blaze-specific activities (from source):
→Being a marketing partner or affiliate — going to your network with existing products
→Sharing the message and meeting new customers at events or conferences
→Speaking to existing customers to find out what they need and want to buy
→Creating partnerships with others who can add value through their network or products
2
Who would you be most passionate working with on this path?
›
What people will you have gotten to know in your future vision? What is the industry you are most passionate about? Who are the leaders in the industry you would love to work with and learn from?
"Make a list of at least 10 people and companies. Research. Ask around. Each person you talk to will know others you may not know about. Don't assume you already know it all — go out with fresh curiosity."
Look for those where you have a TRUSTED CONNECTION — either they know you or they know someone you know. Make sure each is in flow and making money with an opportunity to make more.
"Narrow it down to the top three and focus on them. It isn't a matter of WHETHER it happens — it is just a matter of WHEN."
3
What is the need they have that you can fulfill?
›
Have a look at what opportunities each of your top three is already offering. Find out what their biggest problems and opportunities are. Find out what they need and how it links with your criteria.
"Tell them you love what they do and are committed to being of value to support them — and willing to prove yourself. What would they love to see different in a perfect world? What are their biggest challenges? Where do they see themselves a year from now?"
Do NOT offer solutions yet. Just listen. Then write a list of at least 3 things you believe you can help them with — and work out what that would mean to them in extra revenue or profit.
4
How can you deliver value with your genius to grow their flow?
›
The time has come to put your plan into action and put a trial in place. Narrow to ONE company. Focus all efforts on them for the trial. Be prepared to work for nothing first to prove yourself.
"One month trial — a good start. Use your Superman time. What could you achieve in a one-month trial? How much revenue or profit will you aim to help them make? The first step is to make people know you are committed to serving them."
"Based on your success, what level of results would you need to achieve to earn what you need to earn? Through helping them, they will help you achieve that. More often than not, people who commit to this process end up with the company deciding to find a role and pay them."
5
How will they and you profit from your partnership?
›
Every company is willing to pay something to a partner or contractor who is able to bring them new business or improve their revenues or profits.
Revenue splits: Online businesses with electronic products — up to 70% to affiliates. Service businesses — up to 50%. Product businesses — 5–20%. Asset-based businesses (property) — far less.
Agree on the basics: What are you going to do? What is the benefit to them? What happens if you are successful? What are you waiting for — keep yourself positive with Clark Kent time and answer these five questions to create the job that gets you on the inside.
