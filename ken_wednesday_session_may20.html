2
0:30 — 1:45 · Core Work
The Life Wheel — Baseline Assessment
Score all 8 dimensions. Let the conversation lead. The wheel follows.
The Work
Don't hand him a form. Have a conversation that naturally surfaces where each dimension sits. You score as you listen — he never feels like he's being assessed. The wheel fills in through dialogue.
◎
The Entry Point — Inner Life First
His Inner Life dimension is where the real conversation lives. His energy score has been dropping while Career/Mission climbs. Start here — not with Financial, not with Health. The depth question opens everything else.
Dimension 1 — Inner Life & Spirituality · OPEN HERE
"When did you last feel genuinely purposeful — not productive, but purposeful? Like what you were doing actually mattered in a way you felt, not just knew?"
If he struggles to answer — that IS the answer. Don't fill the silence. Let it sit.
Dimension 2 — Health & Vitality
"How alive do you feel in your body right now — not how fit, not how disciplined. How alive?"
Distinction matters. Fitness is a metric. Aliveness is a feeling. Push on the difference if he defaults to workout stats.
Dimension 3 — Relationships
"How deeply do the people closest to you actually know you right now? Not who you've been — who you are in this season?"
Listen for Caroline. Listen for isolation patterns. Listen for relationships that are maintained vs. relationships that are deepening.
Dimension 4 — Financial
"Does money still motivate you — or does it feel more like scorekeeping now?"
He'll likely score this high. The interesting data is in the nuance of WHY. Post-exit men often have high financial scores and hollow feelings around it.
Dimension 5 — Career / Mission
"What are you building next — in one honest sentence? Not what you're supposed to be building. What you actually want to build."
Watch for the builder's energy here. He'll light up. That's real data. Don't suppress it — ask what about it makes him feel most alive.
Dimension 6 — Experiences & Adventure
"What's one experience you've been meaning to have for over a year that you keep not making happen?"
This one often surfaces what he actually wants vs. what he's been optimizing for. Take the answer seriously — it's a window into the depth phase.
Dimension 7 — Contribution & Legacy
"Who are you genuinely building something with right now — not just advising, not just socializing, but actually co-creating?"
If the answer is nobody — that's important. Post-exit isolation often shows up here first.
Dimension 8 — Fun / Play
"When's the last time you did something purely because it made you feel free — no ROI, no networking, no reason?"
Save this one for last. It's the lightest but often the most revealing. High-performer men almost always score this lowest and know exactly why when they say it out loud.
The North Star Question — Use It If the Room Goes Quiet
"Is your life meaningfully different now than it was six months ago — not busier, not more accomplished. Meaningfully different?"
Ken asked himself this and the answer was no. He knows this question. Let him sit with it again in the context of what you've just covered.

✦ How to Introduce This to Ken
The Pitch
Not a pitch. A conversation. The way you introduce Atlas determines whether Ken owns it or tolerates it. These words matter.
What to Say
How to introduce Atlas
The One-Sentence Frame
"I want to build you a second brain — something that knows your life as well as you do and helps you stay ahead of it instead of reacting to it."
Don't say "AI agent." Don't say "tool." Say second brain. That's what it is.
The Experiment Frame
"I'm not asking you to commit to anything. Try it for 30 days. If it doesn't feel useful, we shut it down. But I think by week three you're going to wonder how you operated without it."
Ken is an experimenter. Always frame new things as experiments. Never permanent. Never pressure.
The Proof Point
"Everything you tell me in this conversation — I want to make sure it doesn't disappear. It becomes the foundation of a living document of your life. One year from now, we'll be able to look back and see exactly what moved and what didn't."
This is what sold Ken on the operating file concept initially. Lead with permanence and progress visibility.

What Ken Cares About — Lead With This
"Is my life meaningfully different?" That's his question. Atlas exists to make the answer yes. Not through more productivity — through more intentional living. Frame Atlas as the system that makes sure he's living by design, not by reaction.
